How Survivor Detection Works: Step-by-Step

From raw overhead distortion to topological grid coordinate snapping in four systematic computer vision stages.

Stage 01
📡

QoS Latched Frame Capture

Subscribes to /image_raw with Reliability: RELIABLE and Durability: TRANSIENT_LOCAL. Receives the single latched frame published by the MuJoCo simulation bridge, then drops the subscription to avoid CPU overhead.

rclpy.qos.QoSProfile(depth=1)
Stage 02
📐

ArUco Homography Rectification

Detects 4 outer DICT_4X4_250 ArUco markers (80, 85, 90, 95). Computes inner boundary corners closest to the arena centroid and computes a $3 \times 3$ perspective transform $\mathbf{M}$ to warp the arena into a canonical $900 \times 900$ pixel coordinate frame.

cv2.getPerspectiveTransform(src, dst)
Stage 03
🔴

Dual-Range Red HSV Masking

Red hue wraps across the $0^\circ / 180^\circ$ boundary. Combines lower band $[0, 10]$ and upper band $[160, 180]$ with morphological opening (3×3 kernel) to isolate red triangular critical markers while completely filtering blue background and green trees.

cv2.bitwise_or(mask_low, mask_high)
Stage 04
🎯

Moments Centroid & Grid Snapping

Calculates spatial moment centroids $(c_x, c_y) = (M_{10}/M_{00}, M_{01}/M_{00})$ for contours with $30 \le \text{Area} \le 6000$. Snaps pixels to the $11 \times 11$ lattice using cell interval $\Delta = 75\text{px}$: $\text{col} = \lfloor c_x/75 + 0.5 \rfloor$, $\text{row} = \lfloor c_y/75 + 0.5 \rfloor$.

map_pixel_to_intersection(cx, cy)

The Trap of Greedy "Nearest Neighbor" vs Optimal TSP

Why the intuitive heuristic fails, and why combinatorial search is the mathematically superior choice.

Greedy Heuristic (Nearest Neighbor)

"Fly to the Closest Survivor Next"

At each step, greedy nearest neighbor makes the locally optimal choice by selecting the nearest unvisited node.

The Trap: In the Task 2C reference layout with survivors C9, D5, H4, J7:
  1. Starts at F6 $(0, 0)$.
  2. D5 is nearest ($2.73\text{m}$), so greedy visits D5 first.
  3. From D5, it visits H4 ($4.87\text{m}$).
  4. From H4, it visits J7 ($4.41\text{m}$).
  5. Now only C9 remains! It is forced to fly a massive diagonal cross across the whole arena ($8.93\text{m}$)!
Total Distance: 20.94 meters (suboptimal!)
Globally Optimal (Brute-Force Open TSP)

"Evaluate All 24 Permutations"

Evaluates the total path length for all $4! = 24$ possible visiting orders and selects the global minimum.

The Mathematical Solution:
  1. Starts at F6 $(0, 0)$.
  2. Flies first to C9 ($4.22\text{m}$) or J7 ($4.94\text{m}$).
  3. Visits C9 → D5 → H4 → J7 in a smooth convex trajectory around the perimeter!
  4. Never crosses back over the center.
  5. Evaluates in < 0.05 milliseconds on any CPU.
Total Distance: 19.38 meters (Saves 1.56m / ~8 seconds!)

Why Intermediate Intersections are Optional

The mathematical and dynamic proof behind flying straight Euclidean 3D vectors.

1. The Triangle Inequality

In any normed vector space $(\mathbb{R}^2, \|\cdot\|_2)$, for any two survivor points $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{c}$, and any intermediate grid intersection $\mathbf{b}$:

$$\|\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{c}\|_2 \le \|\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\|_2 + \|\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{c}\|_2$$

Routing through intermediate grid intersections (Manhattan grid traversal) strictly increases total trajectory length: $\|\mathbf{x}\|_1 \ge \|\mathbf{x}\|_2$.

2. Flight Dynamics & Settling Penalty

In autonomous quadrotor control, introducing intermediate waypoints introduces significant settling latency:

  • Each intermediate waypoint commands the drone's position controller to slow down and align.
  • Even without a hover requirement, velocity transitions induce overshoot and recovery time.
  • Flying direct vectors allows the cascaded PID controller to maintain smooth forward momentum, completing the entire mission in 33.0 seconds.

Mathematical Foundation

Why Cascaded Control for Altitude (Z)?

Understanding why standard single-loop PID fails on drone altitude, and how the dual-loop architecture solves vertical oscillations.

×

The Single-Loop Problem

In quadrotors, the vertical axis is heavily affected by gravity and battery voltage sag. When an aggressive position error occurs, a single PID loop calculates a high throttle value. Because the drone cannot decelerate instantly (it must wait for gravity and drag), it consistently overshoots the target setpoint, entering undamped vertical oscillations ("yo-yo" effect).

Output = Kp × (Z_sp - Z) → Violent overshoots & floor collisions
✓

The Cascaded Solution (In pico_controller_PID.cpp)

By decoupling height error into two nested loops, the outer loop only requests a realistic target velocity ($v_{z,\text{des}}$) capped at 3 m/s. The inner loop acts as an automatic brake: if the drone moves upwards too fast, the velocity error turns negative and instantly reduces thrust before overshoot occurs.

Outer Loop: v_z_des = std::clamp(Kp_pos × e_z + ..., -3.0, 3.0)
Inner Loop: Throttle = Kp_vel × (v_z_des - actual_vel_z)
